\section{Extended protocol}
\label{sec:s1}

The design is a fixed 66-run mixture design over the five-component weight simplex: the union of the $\{5,3\}$ and
$\{5,2\}$ simplex lattices, the overall centroid, the 5 axial points, the 5 quaternary centroids and the 10 midpoints
between the overall centroid and the ternary centroids. It therefore contains the 5 pure vertices, all 10 binary
edges, the ternary and quaternary faces, and uniform voting.

External validation uses 100 compositions per replication that are not in the design and never used for fitting:
60 from $\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\mathbf{1})$ (seed offset $+1000$) and 40 from $\mathrm{Dirichlet}(0.5\cdot\mathbf{1})$
(offset $+2000$).

Every stochastic component is seeded as $20260904 + r$ for replication $r$: the outer stratified partition, the
inner fold assignment, the model random states, the Dirichlet validation compositions (offsets $+1000$ and $+2000$),
the direct AUC search ($+3000$, $+4000$), the empirical reference ($+5000$ onwards), the comparators ($+12000$),
quality scoring ($+13000$) and NSGA-II ($+770000$). The seed policy, the full seed map, the configuration history and the resolved library
versions are in \texttt{reports/\allowbreak pco213\_postwork\_benchmark/\allowbreak manifests/\allowbreak benchmark\_manifest.json}; per-dataset
provenance --- source, acquisition command, the expected row, feature and positive-class-prevalence values, and the
SHA-256 checksum of the file actually used --- is in the corresponding
\texttt{*\_dataset\_manifest.json}.

The statistical analysis plan, fixed before the replicated benchmark and committed to version control, is
\texttt{STATISTICAL\_ANALYSIS\_R30.md}.

\paragraph{Runner, checkpointing and execution provenance.}
The benchmark is driven by a single checkpointed runner
(\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak pco213\_\allowbreak run\_\allowbreak postwork\_\allowbreak benchmark.py}) with ten
stages per replication: out-of-fold estimation, mixture design, Scheff\'e fitting, single-objective references,
empirical Pareto reference, NBI-A, NBI-B, NBI-C, comparators and quality scoring. State is recorded per dataset
$\times$ replication $\times$ stage, so an interrupted run resumes without recomputation and never silently
recomputes a completed stage. A dry-run mode lists pending work; it was used to verify, before the extension was
launched, that replications 0--9 were complete and that exactly 80 replications (800 stages) were pending.
Aggregation and statistics are separate scripts
(\texttt{pco213\_\allowbreak postwork\_\allowbreak benchmark\_\allowbreak report.py} and
\texttt{\ldots\_\allowbreak stats.py}), so the analysis can be rerun without re-executing the experiment. The
library has 83 unit tests, including reference implementations checked against scikit-learn, exact hypervolume on
known cases, the 66-run design, NBI anchor and warm-start behaviour, and the resumption planner, and the deterministic simplex repair and budget arithmetic of the NSGA-II baseline.

Execution consumed 81.1 hours of stage time (27.5 for the first ten replications, 53.6 for the extension) and
produced 3{,}600 model fits, 19{,}920 design evaluations, 23{,}760 NBI subproblems, $5.02\times10^{7}$ real
objective evaluations, $1.55\times10^{7}$ reference points and $4.8\times10^{6}$ direct-AUC-search evaluations, on
the environment pinned in the main text. Two stages were retried, both in the first ten replications; none in the
extension. The per-replication raw artifacts that are not version-controlled are the out-of-fold probability
matrices, the candidate sets and the reference samples.

\paragraph{The BNP Paribas collapse, in full.} NBI-A's weighted hypervolume ratio splits into 21 partitions at
0.891--0.928, two intermediate, and seven collapsing to 0.036--0.120: partitions 7, 9, 18, 20, 23, 26 and 27. The
seven coincide exactly with the partitions in which the parsimony rule selects the quadratic ROC-AUC order (7 of 7,

seven collapses the surrogate anchor carries $k$-nearest neighbours and costs 16--31~ms per 1{,}000 rows, giving

cost is described as a marker rather than the mechanism.

\section{NBI solver diagnostics}
\label{sec:s7}
\input{tables/s7_nbi_runs}

\paragraph{Success semantics are not on a common scale.} NBI-A and NBI-B optimize smooth polynomials and a subproblem
counts as successful only when SLSQP certifies optimality. NBI-C optimizes a piecewise-constant ROC-AUC, where such
certification is generically unattainable, so a subproblem is accepted when its equality residual falls below
$10^{-3}$, under a reduced budget. The UCI credit gap between NBI-C and NBI-B is substantially a convergence effect
of NBI-B, which certifies a median 29 of 66 subproblems there; retaining its unconverged iterates preserves the
direction but reduces the mean gap from $+0.277$ to $+0.108$ hypervolume, and in the three partitions where NBI-B
converges fully the gap is at most 0.01.

\section{Empirical reference convergence}
\label{sec:s8}
\input{tables/s8_reference}

The reference has two layers. The \emph{sampled core} is built from Dirichlet samples, the simplex lattice, all
vertices and edges, an $\varepsilon$-constraint sweep, the 66 design runs and the single-objective references; it
contains the search output of none of the five compared methods. The \emph{final augmented reference} is the
non-dominated union of that core with every candidate set. Because NBI-B and NBI-C contribute 30--40\% of its points,
and NSGA-II 8--29\%, absolute indicator values computed against the augmented reference are partly self-graded, which
is why every primary comparison is repeated against the sample core (Section~\ref{sec:s13}).

\section{Cost definition and the accuracy--calibration conflict}
\label{sec:s9}
\input{tables/s9_cost_sensitivity}
\input{tables/s9_conflict}

\paragraph{The accuracy--calibration conflict in weight space, and the Santander cost artifact.} The ROC-AUC
advantage of the direct optimum over the SLSQP log-loss optimum has bootstrap interval half-widths below 0.00004 on
every dataset. The two optima nonetheless differ in weight space: $L_1$ distances 0.35, 0.38, 0.06 and 0.20
(Santander, BNP Paribas, Porto Seguro, UCI credit), the Porto Seguro pair practically coinciding, with support
Jaccard 0.50 to 0.97. The apparent $+254$~ms per 1{,}000 rows Santander deployment-cost gap between the two optima is
an activity-threshold artifact: the ROC-AUC optimum retains a $k$-nearest-neighbour weight just above $10^{-3}$ that
is AUC-inert (zeroing it changes out-of-fold ROC-AUC by $3 \times 10^{-6}$ on average), and an AUC-equivalent blend
without $k$-nearest neighbours costs about $+4$~ms per 1{,}000 rows. No deployment-cost gap between the two optima is
claimed. Under the two cost definitions the hypervolume-best method changes in 10 of 30 partitions on Santander, 24 of
30 on BNP Paribas, 8 of 30 on Porto Seguro and 20 of 30 on UCI credit.

\paragraph{The BNP Paribas support-cost normalization artifact.} NBI-C's support-cost hypervolume appears bimodal,
but this is a per-replication normalization effect, not a regime. The cost bound of the normalization box is
approximately 17~ms per 1{,}000 rows in 13 partitions, where no $k$-nearest-neighbour-bearing point survives on the
support reference front, and 160--167 in the other 17, a tenfold rescaling of one axis. NBI-C's fronts are the same
in both groups. Under a common bound the split disappears. The robust statement is qualitative: NBI sets optimized
under the linear cost omit the cheapest supports that the raw design contains.

\section{Holdout transfer}
\label{sec:s10}
\input{tables/s10_holdout}

\paragraph{Ranking agreement by batch.} The out-of-fold-best of the four knee-selected picks is also holdout-best in
30/30 partitions on Santander, 15/30 on BNP Paribas, 23/30 on Porto Seguro and 30/30 on UCI credit; restricted to the
twenty partitions added in the extension the counts are 20/20, 10/20, 15/20 and 20/20, reproducing the pattern
exactly.

\paragraph{Knee-selected solutions compared across arms.} Pairing the knee-selected solution of two arms by partition
under the weighted cost, the two contrasts that carry the only Holm-significant primary results have worse
single-objective holdout accuracy at the selected point: Santander NBI-B minus NBI-A is $-0.0047$ holdout ROC-AUC,
better in 15 of 30 partitions, with holdout log-loss worse in 16 of 30; UCI credit NBI-C minus NBI-B is $-0.0119$,
better in 6 of 30, with log-loss worse in 24 of 30. Both selected points are also materially cheaper, by $0.97$ and
$2.84$~ms per 1{,}000 rows. The two contrasts in which the selected point does improve are BNP Paribas NBI-B minus
NBI-A ($+0.0029$, better in 28 of 30) and Santander NBI-C minus NBI-B ($+0.0016$, better in 26 of 30) --- the
contrasts with the smaller indicator effects.

\paragraph{How much there is to win at all.} The gain of the log-loss optimum over the best single model on the
untouched holdout is $+0.0076$, $+0.0038$, $+0.0053$ and $+0.0005$ ROC-AUC on Santander, BNP Paribas, Porto Seguro
and UCI credit, better than the best single model in 30, 30, 29 and 23 of 30 partitions. On UCI credit the
out-of-fold gain is $+0.0007$ and the overlap-corrected $p$ is $0.24$: on that dataset no weighting method in this
study separates from the best single model on held-out data.

\section{From ten to thirty replications}
\label{sec:s11}
\input{tables/s11_r10_r30}

\paragraph{What tripling the replications changed.} Three findings changed status between $R = 10$ and $R = 30$. The
Porto Seguro and UCI credit anchor effect went from an undecidable 6/10 and 5/10 with one tie to 24/30 with bootstrap
intervals excluding zero; the BNP Paribas anchor collapse became a quantified rate of 7/30 with an identified
trigger, selection of the quadratic ROC-AUC order; and the BNP Paribas support-cost bimodality and the Santander
deployment-cost gap were exposed as artifacts (Sections~\ref{sec:s9} and~\ref{sec:s6}). In the extension batch alone
the anchor win rates on hypervolume are 18/20 on Porto Seguro and 19/20 on UCI credit.

Against the twenty new partitions alone, rather than the nested $R = 30$ sample, only 6 of 12 hypervolume and 5 of 12
IGD$^+$ cells agree with the $R = 10$ estimate, and the Porto Seguro and UCI credit anchor win rates differ between
batches under an identical protocol (UCI credit Fisher $p = 0.009$). That disagreement is not a defect of either
batch; it is the magnitude of partition sensitivity the extension was designed to measure.

\section{Anchor-injection control}
\label{sec:s12}
\input{tables/s12_anchor_injection}

Because a vertex value of $\beta$ returns its anchor directly, NBI-B's returned set contains the three real
single-objective optima, which are also points of the empirical reference. Part of the NBI-B advantage is therefore
the injection of those extreme solutions rather than the relocation of the CHIM. Rescoring NBI-A's own candidate set

\section{Corrections log}
\label{sec:s14}

Readings that appeared in earlier analyses of this study and did not survive replication or adversarial verification,
with the evidence that overturned each. They are listed because each is traceable in the repository history and a
reader should be able to see what changed.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The BNP Paribas support-cost bimodality of NBI-C} was reported at $R = 10$ as a regime. It is a
per-replication normalization-box artifact (Section~\ref{sec:s9}).
\item \textbf{A Santander deployment-cost gap between the AUC and log-loss optima} was attributed to the AUC optimum.
It is an activity-threshold artifact: the derivative-free search leaves a $k$-nearest-neighbour weight just above
$10^{-3}$ that is AUC-inert, and an AUC-equivalent blend without it costs about $+4$~ms per 1{,}000 rows.
\item \textbf{The random Dirichlet comparator was described as budget-matched.} It is matched only in the number of
returned candidates and is degenerate under the weighted cost; it is a floor check.
\item \textbf{Uniform spacing was treated as an advantage of NBI.} It does not survive revalidation, and the
NSGA-II arm shows the shortfall is a property of the CHIM construction rather than of the objective geometry.
\item \textbf{The gate-conditional anchor gain on BNP Paribas} was read as evidence that real anchors add more when
the surface is reliable. It restates NBI-A's collapse, since three of the five gate-passing partitions are collapse
partitions.
\item \textbf{The A-versus-B contrast was described as a clean single-factor experiment.} It is not: replacing the
anchors also moves the utopia point, rescales both normalization axes, reorients the CHIM and substitutes three
returned candidates (Section~\ref{sec:s12}).
\item \textbf{The compute premium was quoted from solver stages only.} Charged standalone, so that each arm carries
the stages it depends on, the NBI-C premium over NBI-B is 2.3--6.2$\times$, not 3--73$\times$.
\end{enumerate}
