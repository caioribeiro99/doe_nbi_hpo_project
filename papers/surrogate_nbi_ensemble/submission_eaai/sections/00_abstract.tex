Choosing soft-voting ensemble weights is a mixture experiment already addressed by an established mixture-design,
response-surface and Normal Boundary Intersection pipeline. The artificial-intelligence contribution is a diagnosis
of when that pipeline can be trusted, not a new method; the engineering application is designing weighted classifier
ensembles under a measured deployment cost. Three arms differing in objective and anchor sources ran on 30
overlapping, paired partitions of four public financial-services datasets, with controls the framework lacks: a
reliability gate fixed beforehand, real-objective revalidation, a reference whose sampled core contains no output of
the compared methods, and an untouched holdout. Gate passes ranged from 0 to 30 of 30 partitions by dataset and
metric. Under the continuous cost the arms optimize, real anchors beat surrogate ones on both primary endpoints in
every partition of the two datasets whose surfaces for the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve
almost never passed, and in 23--24 of 30 elsewhere (significant after correction only on Santander). On those two
datasets anchor misplacement was the largest observed failure; a control traced most of it to injected extreme
solutions. The gate detected unusable surfaces but not misplaced anchors. Continuous and step deployment costs
disagreed on the winning candidate set in 8--24 of 30 partitions and on the anchor effect's direction on one dataset.
The non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II), given about twice the metamodel-free arm's weight-vector
evaluations, outperformed it. The diagnosis concerns one-shot pipelines without infill; four datasets establish a
pattern, not a general rule.
