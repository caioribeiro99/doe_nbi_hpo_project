\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The protocol was fixed before the replicated study and was not changed after any result was observed; the extension
from 10 to 30 replications reused it without modification, and \cref{sec:results:r30} reports what that extension
changed.

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:protocol:data}

Four public binary tabular classification problems span prevalence, dimensionality and feature type
(\cref{tab:datasets}): Santander (high-dimensional, anonymized, numeric, 10\% positives); BNP Paribas (numeric plus
high-cardinality categorical features, 76\% positive); Porto Seguro (strongly imbalanced at 3.6\% positives,
subsampled to 200{,}000 rows by a single stratified draw with a recorded seed to bound runtime); and UCI credit
default (small and low-dimensional). For the three Kaggle competition datasets the competition test labels are not
public and were never used: all training, weighting, thresholding and model selection use only the published training
file, from which every performance figure in this paper is derived.

\subsection{Base models and leakage control}
\label{sec:protocol:models}

The ensemble contains $M = 5$ deliberately heterogeneous learners with fixed hyperparameters and no hyperparameter
search: $\ell_2$-regularized logistic regression; Gaussian naive Bayes; $k$-nearest neighbours with $k = 100$; a
random forest with 200 trees and a minimum of 20 samples per leaf; and gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost, 400 rounds,
depth 6, learning rate 0.1, subsample and column subsample 0.8). The zoo spans the accuracy--cost--calibration space
rather than forming a competitive stack: it deliberately includes a member strong in ranking but poorly calibrated
(Gaussian naive Bayes) and one accurate but two orders of magnitude more expensive at inference time ($k$-nearest
neighbours). Preprocessing --- imputation, standardization and encoding, detailed per model in the supplement --- is
fitted inside each training fold and never on validation data.

Each replication splits the dataset by a stratified 80/20 partition into a training part and an untouched holdout.
Within the training part, stratified 5-fold cross-validation produces out-of-fold probability estimates $\mathbf{P}$
for all five models; these are the only quantities the design, the surfaces, the optimizers and the selection rules
ever see. Each model is then refitted once on the whole training part to produce holdout probabilities $\mathbf{Q}$,
used exclusively for the confirmation analysis of \cref{sec:results:holdout}. Because \cref{eq:ensemble} is linear in
$\wvec$, evaluating any weight vector costs one matrix--vector product, which is what makes an empirical reference of
$10^{5}$ points and a metamodel-free NBI arm affordable.

Inference cost $c_i$ is measured, not assumed: per model and replication, the median of five timed
\texttt{predict\_proba} calls on a fixed 10{,}000-row batch, including preprocessing, in milliseconds per 1{,}000 rows.

\subsection{Replication, seeds and the statistical unit}
\label{sec:protocol:replication}

The entire pipeline, from the outer partition through scoring, is executed $R = 30$ times per dataset, giving 120
replications, with the outer partition, the inner folds and every stochastic component seeded by
$\text{20260904} + r$ for replication $r$. All seeds are persisted in the manifest, and the checkpointed runner of
\cref{sec:repro} recomputes any stage exactly.

The statistical unit is \textbf{one outer partition of one dataset}. Because all methods are evaluated on the same
partition, every comparison is \emph{paired}. Two consequences constrain the analysis throughout.

First, the 30 partitions of a dataset overlap heavily: any two training parts share on average 75\% of their rows.
The replication distribution therefore measures \emph{sensitivity to data partitioning}, not sampling from a
population, and the effective information is far below 30 independent runs. Second, and consequently, the dataset is
the unit of generalization: results are summarized per dataset and the four dataset-level results are then compared.
Nothing is pooled across the $4 \times 30$ replications, and no statement of the form ``$N = 120$ independent
experiments'' is made anywhere in this paper.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
\label{sec:protocol:stats}

The primary comparisons were fixed before the replicated benchmark and committed to version control: NBI-B versus
NBI-A, NBI-C versus NBI-B, NBI-C versus random scalarization, and NBI-C versus the random Dirichlet sample. The
primary endpoints are $\igdp$ and the hypervolume ratio, computed on revalidated objectives against the empirical
reference of the same replication, under the weighted cost that the optimizers actually minimized; the support-cost
repetition is a pre-specified sensitivity analysis. All other indicators are descriptive.

Paired differences are signed so that $\Delta > 0$ always favours the second-named method:
$\Delta\hv = \hv_{\text{new}} - \hv_{\text{ref}}$ and
$\Delta\igdp = \igdp_{\text{ref}} - \igdp_{\text{new}}$. For each dataset $\times$ comparison $\times$ endpoint we
report the mean and median with percentile-bootstrap 95\% intervals (10{,}000 resamples, fixed seed), the quartiles
and range, the win/tie/loss counts with a tie tolerance of $10^{-4}$, the win fraction with a Wilson 95\% interval,
and the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation as a distribution-free effect size.

An ordinary paired $t$-test on resampled partitions is anticonservative, so the primary test is the Nadeau--Bengio
corrected resampled $t$-test \citep{nadeau2003inference}: with $J = 30$ paired differences $d_j$,
\begin{equation}
t \;=\; \frac{\bar d}{\sqrt{\operatorname{var}(d)\left(\tfrac{1}{J} + \rho\right)}}, \qquad
\rho \;=\; \frac{n_{\text{test}}}{n_{\text{train}}} \;=\; \frac{0.20}{0.80} \;=\; 0.25,
\label{eq:nb}
\end{equation}
on $J - 1$ degrees of freedom. The correction inflates the variance by a factor of
$(1/30 + 0.25)/(1/30) \approx 8.5$, multiplying standard errors by about 2.9 relative to the naive test. Within each
dataset the eight primary tests form one family and receive a Holm correction; no correction is applied across
datasets, because datasets are not pooled. A Wilcoxon signed-rank test is reported alongside as a distribution-free
check, but it is not corrected for overlap and cannot rescue a non-significant corrected result.

Where a paired-difference distribution is visibly bimodal or heavy-tailed, the mean-based test is uninformative by
construction and we say so, reporting the median with its bootstrap interval, the win fraction with its Wilson
interval and the rank-biserial correlation as the evidence instead. Throughout, effect consistency takes precedence
over $p$-values. Frequencies over replications are reported with Wilson and Jeffreys 95\% intervals.
