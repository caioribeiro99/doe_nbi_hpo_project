Choosing soft-voting ensemble weights is a mixture experiment on a simplex, already addressed by an established
mixture-design, response-surface and Normal Boundary Intersection pipeline. We ask not whether that pipeline yields a
Pareto front, but when the front can be trusted. Three arms defined by two methodological factors --- the source of
the objectives and the source of the anchors --- run over four public binary tabular datasets $\times$ 30 partitions, paired
and overlap-corrected, behind controls the framework lacks: a pre-specified external reliability gate; revalidation of
every candidate on the real objectives; an empirical reference whose sampled core is constructed independently of the
surrogate and then augmented with all candidate sets; and an untouched holdout. Surrogate adequacy is dataset- and
metric-dependent: ROC-AUC surfaces pass the gate in 0, 5, 16 and 30 of 30 partitions, log-loss in 30, 3, 12 and 30.
Among failures attributable to the surrogate pipeline, anchor misplacement has the largest observed effect: real
anchors improve both primary endpoints in all 30 partitions of the two datasets whose surfaces fail, in 23--24 of 30
on Porto Seguro and 24 of 30 on UCI credit. A control injecting only those anchors attributes most of that gap to the
returned extreme solutions on the first two, and to the relocated construction on UCI credit, where subproblem
convergence, not surrogate error, dominates. The gate detects unusable surfaces but not misplaced anchors. Continuous
and step deployment costs disagree about which of six candidate sets wins in 10 to 24 of 30 partitions; at a matched
budget NSGA-II beats the metamodel-free arm. The diagnosis concerns one-shot, no-infill pipelines; four datasets give
a four-point cross-dataset pattern, not a tested claim.
