Choosing soft-voting ensemble weights is a mixture experiment on a simplex, already addressed by an established
mixture-design, response-surface and Normal Boundary Intersection pipeline. The artificial-intelligence contribution
here is not that pipeline but a diagnosis of when it can be trusted; the engineering application is the design of
weighted classifier ensembles under a measured deployment cost. Three arms, defined by the source of the objectives and
the source of the anchors, run over four public binary tabular datasets $\times$ 30 overlapping partitions, paired by
partition, behind controls the framework lacks: a pre-specified external reliability gate; revalidation of
every candidate on the real objectives; an empirical reference whose sampled core contains no output of the
compared methods; and an untouched holdout. Surrogate adequacy is dataset- and metric-dependent: surfaces for the
area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) pass the gate in 0, 5, 16 and 30 of 30 partitions,
and log-loss surfaces in 30, 3, 12 and 30. Under the continuous cost the arms optimize, replacing surrogate anchors
with real ones improves both primary endpoints in all 30 partitions of the two datasets whose ROC-AUC surfaces almost
never pass the gate, and in 23 to 24 of 30 elsewhere (significant after correction only on Santander). On those two
datasets, anchor misplacement has the largest observed effect among failures attributable to the surrogate pipeline,
and a control attributes most of that effect to the injected extreme solutions rather than to the relocated
construction. The gate detects unusable surfaces but not misplaced anchors. Continuous and step deployment costs
disagree about the winning candidate set in 8 to 24 of 30 partitions and about the direction of the anchor effect on
one dataset, and the non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm NSGA-II, given about twice the metamodel-free arm's
weight-vector evaluations, beats that arm.
The diagnosis concerns one-shot pipelines without infill, and four datasets give a pattern rather than a tested
claim.
