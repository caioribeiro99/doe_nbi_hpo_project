Choosing soft-voting ensemble weights is a mixture experiment on a simplex, already addressed by an established
mixture-design, response-surface and Normal Boundary Intersection pipeline. The artificial-intelligence contribution
here is not that pipeline but a diagnosis of when it can be trusted; the engineering application is the design of
weighted classifier ensembles under a measured deployment cost. Three arms, defined by the source of the objectives and
the source of the anchors, run over four public binary tabular datasets $\times$ 30 partitions, paired and
overlap-corrected, behind controls the framework lacks: a pre-specified external reliability gate; revalidation of
every candidate on the real objectives; an empirical reference whose sampled core is built independently of the
surrogate; and an untouched holdout. Surrogate adequacy is dataset- and metric-dependent: surfaces for the
area under the receiver operating characteristic curve pass the gate in 0, 5, 16 and 30 of 30 partitions, and
log-loss surfaces in 30, 3, 12 and 30. Among failures attributable to the surrogate pipeline, anchor misplacement has
the largest observed effect, improving both primary endpoints in all 30 partitions of the two datasets whose surfaces
fail and in 23 to 24 of 30 elsewhere; a control attributes most of that gap to the injected extreme
solutions rather than to the relocated construction. The gate detects unusable surfaces but not misplaced
anchors. Continuous and step deployment costs disagree about the winning candidate set in 10 to 24 of 30 partitions,
and at a matched evaluation budget the non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm NSGA-II beats the metamodel-free arm.
The diagnosis concerns one-shot pipelines without infill, and four datasets give a pattern rather than a tested
claim.
